## The problem

Your org chart says who reports to whom. It doesn't show who keeps the lights on. The real
work often lives in a few heads, a few inboxes, and a few routines nobody wrote down. Things
run fine — until one person is out, one tool changes, or one vendor disappears. Then you find
out how much was resting on a single point you couldn't see.

## Why org charts miss the real issue

An org chart is a picture of *authority*. It's tidy and top-down. But work doesn't flow along
those lines. It flows through whoever actually knows how to do the thing, whoever everyone
calls when it breaks, and whoever quietly cleans up the gaps. That's the **actual work
chart** — and it's usually invisible to leadership until something fails.

## FAQ

**How long does it take?**
The Work Health Check is typically 2–4 weeks from completed intake, depending on team size
and access. The Signal Map is about a week.

**How much of my team's time does it need?**
Intake and a stakeholder review, plus interviews for the deeper tier. We work from your
documents and a small number of conversations — we keep the load light.

**Is this an HR audit or a staffing plan?**
No. It's an operational diagnostic. It maps dependency and risk in how work gets done. It's
not recruiting, not compliance certification, and not a software build.

**Do you install anything or fix it for us?**
The diagnostic delivers findings and a repair plan. Hands-on fixes can be a separate,
scoped engagement.

**What if we don't have a real org chart or documentation?**
That's common — and it's part of the finding. We map reality either way.

**Will this tell us what to automate?**
It includes an AI/automation readiness review: what's a good candidate, what needs to be
documented or stabilized first, and where automation would reduce risk versus add it.
