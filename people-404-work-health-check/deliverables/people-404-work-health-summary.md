## Executive summary

[Organization] is a [size]-person [industry] company preparing for [AI adoption /
growth / restructuring]. Across the three areas reviewed — [Area A], [Area B], and
[Area C] — the work is getting done, but it is increasingly dependent on a small
number of people absorbing hidden operational load.

The core finding: your **human infrastructure is carrying more than the visible
process accounts for**. Two roles have become informal single points of failure,
one critical handoff has no clear owner, and the most important recurring decision
routinely stalls for 3–5 days waiting on a single approver.

None of these are performance problems. They are infrastructure problems. Left in
place, they will constrain any attempt to adopt AI or scale — because you would be
building on a work system that is already at its limit. The 30-day plan at the end
of this summary sequences the highest-leverage fixes first.

**Overall Work Health signal: `ELEVATED STRAIN` — stable today, fragile under change.**

## AI / growth readiness notes

- The reviewed workflows are **not yet ready to automate as-is** — automating them
  would scale the exceptions and the hidden work rather than reduce them.
- Clarifying ownership and documenting the two concentrated knowledge domains are
  prerequisites to any reliable AI or scaling initiative in these areas.

## Priority recommendations

1. **Name owners** for the unowned handoff and the ambiguous decision — this alone
   removes the largest source of latency.
2. **Document the two concentrated knowledge domains** to de-risk single points of failure.
3. **Redistribute exception handling** in [Area A] away from the single overloaded person.
4. **Define decision rights and an escalation path** for [recurring decision].
5. **Re-sequence AI/scaling plans** to follow, not precede, the fixes above.
