Overall Work Health signal: ELEVATED STRAIN — stable today, fragile under change.

Executive summary

A ~90-person professional services firm preparing for AI adoption and steady growth. Across the three areas reviewed, the work is getting done — but increasingly through a small number of people absorbing hidden operational load. Two roles have become informal single points of failure, one critical handoff has no clear owner, and the most important recurring decision routinely stalls for 3–5 days.

These are infrastructure problems, not performance problems. Left in place, they will constrain any attempt to adopt AI or scale, because the work system underneath is already at its limit.

Human infrastructure risks

  • Single points of failure. Two roles hold undocumented knowledge the workflow can't currently run without.
  • Load concentration. Most exceptions route through one person not formally responsible for them.
  • Fragility under change. The system works because people compensate; AI or growth removes that slack.

Role clarity findings

  • Two roles overlap on a shared responsibility, creating duplicated effort and occasional gaps.
  • A critical handoff has no named owner and succeeds only through informal checking.
  • Decision rights for a recurring decision are ambiguous, driving the latency below.

Workflow pressure points

  • The exception stage of the primary workflow exceeds the single handler's capacity.
  • A clean workflow is undermined by an unowned handoff mid-stream.
  • A third area depends on one person with no documented fallback.

Knowledge transfer concerns

  • Critical know-how in two domains is undocumented and held by one person each.
  • Onboarding into these areas is informal and slow.

Decision bottlenecks

  • A recurring decision waits 3–5 days for a single approver, stalling downstream work.
  • Escalation paths are unclear, so work pauses rather than reroutes.

Burnout risk indicators

Two individuals consistently absorb above-scope load and are load-bearing for the reviewed areas. Sustained compensation of this kind is a leading indicator of turnover and error. Advisory only — not a clinical or medical assessment.

AI / growth readiness notes

  • The reviewed workflows are not yet ready to automate as-is; doing so would scale the exceptions and hidden work.
  • Clarifying ownership and documenting the concentrated knowledge domains are prerequisites to reliable AI or scaling.

Priority recommendations

  1. Name owners for the unowned handoff and the ambiguous decision.
  2. Document the two concentrated knowledge domains.
  3. Redistribute exception handling away from the single overloaded person.
  4. Define decision rights and an escalation path.
  5. Re-sequence AI/scaling plans to follow, not precede, these fixes.
